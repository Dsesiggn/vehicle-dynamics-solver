Principles, definitions & limits ＋

Rigid steering linkage

Each upright rotates about the line through its upper and lower steering-axis points. A constant-length tie rod joins the rotating outer joint to an inner joint translated by the rack. The solution follows the assembly branch connected to the entered static geometry.

rack = gain × steering-wheel angle / 360
‖outer(θ) − inner(rack)‖ = static tie-rod length

Positions and lengths use millimeters; displayed angles use degrees. Both rack ends translate by the same signed displacement in +Y. Wheel centers and spindle directions rotate with their upright.

The closure uses all three coordinates: unequal inner/outer tie-rod heights and inclined steering arms/axes are included. The rack travel direction itself is fixed to +Y; a tilted rack travel axis requires a different input and actuation model.

Rack offset from the front axle

rack offset = (left inner X + right inner X) / 2
ΔX = requested offset − current offset
new inner X = old inner X + ΔX · on both sides

Positive offset is ahead of the front axle; negative is behind, in millimeters. Applying an offset preserves the existing left/right X difference. If an X coordinate is unknown, the explicitly labeled “Set both rack X values” action places both at the entered offset. The hardpoints remain the stored geometry; no duplicate offset is saved. This layout edit recalculates neutral tie-rod lengths and the steering sweep. Rack stroke during steering still acts only in +Y.

Nominal Ackermann benchmark

cot(δoutside) − cot(δinside) = front track / wheelbase

This is the planar, zero-slip rolling condition for nominal wheel centers and a fixed, straight rear axle. The ideal outside angle is calculated at the solved inside angle. The error is actual outside minus ideal outside. Rear track sets rear-wheel placement; it does not enter this front-angle relation.

Ackermann % = 100 × (wheelbase / front track) × [cot(δoutside) − cot(δinside)]

This cotangent definition is 100% for the nominal ideal and 0% for parallel headings; it differs from steering-arm intersection percentages in the references. It uses absolute solved headings, including static toe. The percentage is omitted if either angle is below 0.01°, or for straight, opposing, or ≥89° headings. No percentage is inferred at center.

The dashed reference is a heading comparison, not a solved vehicle turn center. Tilted steering axes, wheel-center offsets and camber can move the actual contact geometry. A road-contact and jacking solution is outside this model.

Tie-rod / steering-arm angles

Plan angle α is measured at the outer ball joint between the tie rod and the arm line to the steering axis at that ball’s height (Milliken Fig. 19.3, p.714). The dashed arm extensions meet at the displayed plan intersection. This construction is approximate as an Ackermann design guide; it is not the rolling turn center.

Effective 3D angle β uses the perpendicular radius from the steering axis to the outer joint and the spatial tie rod. With inclined axes it uses a different arm reference from α. Both are unsigned included angles, 0–180°. No physical arm shape or ball-joint articulation limit is inferred.

angle = atan2(‖arm ray × rod ray‖, arm ray · rod ray)
Both rays start at the outer tie-rod joint.

Outputs and scope

Heading is positive rightward. Left toe-in equals left heading; right toe-in is negative right heading. Total toe is their sum; Δ toe is the change from the entered static alignment. Inside/outside and ideal comparisons are unavailable when wheel headings oppose or when their turn is not defined.

Fixed chassis and steering axes; rigid joints and links; constant rack gain. The neutral rack is the midpoint of entered lock-to-lock travel, so permitted displacement is ± half that travel. No suspension bump, compliance, backlash, tire slip, tire forces or dynamic load transfer. The synthetic example is a software demonstration, not measured or recommended vehicle geometry.

Source verification, numerical checks and physical validation are distinct. The tool does not establish experimental validity for your vehicle.
